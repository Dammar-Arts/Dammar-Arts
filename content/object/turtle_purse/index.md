Following our first Beaumont purse experiment, we're looking at a rather different design this time: the Turtle.

The idea was simple enough: two connected squares folding into a small coin purse. Getting there was another matter.


![Turtle Purse](turtle.jpg "Turtle")

We started with drawings, then made a paper mockup to work out the folding and proportions. As we experimented, we discovered that the construction required nine separate pieces of leather. Four of these are dedicated to the closure, helping reinforce it while giving it a cleaner finish.

The leather prototype involved quite a bit of trial and error. We glued, stitched, made mistakes and redid parts of the construction before taking the purse out for a practical test at the bar.

That last step was particularly useful. Some problems only become apparent when you actually use an object. We identified several pain points, went back to the desk and made further adjustments.

The result is a small coin purse whose construction turned out to be considerably more complicated than its original drawing suggested.

For Beaumont, we're imagining it as a handmade gift to Léon from a leatherworker grateful for his medical care.
